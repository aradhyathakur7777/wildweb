FIRST, THE BIG IDEA

Wildlife is not just about animals.

When we talk about wildlife, we usually think about animals first. But there is a much bigger picture.

🌎 Everything is connected.

Think about a forest. Plants need sunlight, water and soil. Herbivores eat plants. Predators eat some herbivores. Decomposers break down dead plants and animals.

So if one part of the ecosystem changes, other parts can be affected too.

That is why conserving wildlife does not simply mean saving one animal. It means protecting the whole system around it.

🌳 🦋 🐅
ECOSYSTEM

So... what is an ecosystem?

An ecosystem is made up of living things and non-living things that interact with each other in one place.

🌱

Plants

Plants are producers. They make their own food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. They also become food for many animals.

🐾

Animals

Animals are consumers because they get energy by eating plants, other animals, or both.

🍄

Decomposers

Fungi and many microorganisms break down dead material. This helps nutrients return to the environment.

☀️

Sunlight

The Sun gives energy to plants. Plants then become an energy source for other organisms.

💧

Water

Animals and plants both need water. Rivers, ponds, lakes and rainfall can all be important parts of ecosystems.

🪨

Soil

Soil gives plants support and contains water, air, minerals and many tiny living organisms.

Bengal Tiger

Panthera tigris

The Bengal tiger is a large predator found in parts of South Asia.

🌳 Where does it live?

Bengal tigers can live in forests, grasslands, wetlands and mangrove forests.

🍖 What does it eat?

It is a carnivore. Deer and wild pigs are examples of animals that can become tiger prey.

🔗 What is its role?

The tiger is a top predator in many food webs. It helps keep predator-prey relationships part of the ecosystem.

⚠️ What threatens it?

Habitat loss, habitat fragmentation, poaching and loss of prey can affect wild tiger populations.

Food Carnivore
Habitat Forests, grasslands and wetlands
Gestation Around 3–4 months
Special feature Every tiger has its own stripe pattern.

💡 Did you know?

The stripes on a tiger's skin also continue underneath its fur.

🦌

Spotted Deer

Axis axis

The spotted deer, also called chital, is an important herbivore in many Indian forests.

🌳 Where does it live?

Chital are found in forests and open grassy areas, often near water.

🌿 What does it eat?

It mainly eats grass, leaves, flowers, fruits and other plant material.

🔗 What is its role?

It takes energy from plants and passes some of that energy to predators when it becomes their prey.

⚠️ What can affect it?

Loss of habitat, lack of food or water, and disturbance can affect its population.

Food Herbivore
Special feature White spots on its body
Movement Fast runner and jumper
Role Herbivore and prey

💡 Did you know?

Chital often stay alert and use their senses to notice predators.

🦋

Butterfly

Order: Lepidoptera

Butterflies may look tiny and delicate, but they are connected to many parts of an ecosystem.

🌸 What does it eat?

Adult butterflies often drink nectar from flowers using a long tube-like mouthpart called a proboscis.

🌼 Why are they useful?

While visiting flowers, butterflies can help move pollen between flowers.

🔄 Life cycle

A butterfly goes through four main stages: egg → caterpillar → pupa → adult.

Stages 4
Order Lepidoptera
Caterpillar food Mostly plants
Adult food Mostly nectar

💡 Did you know?

Butterflies have taste receptors on their feet. Yes, their feet can help them sense what they land on! 🦶🦋

🐻

Himalayan Brown Bear

Ursus arctos

This bear lives in high mountain areas of the Himalayan region.

🏔️ Where does it live?

It is associated with high mountain habitats, including alpine areas and places near the tree line.

🍎 What does it eat?

It is an omnivore, so it can eat both plant material and animal food.

🔗 What is its role?

Because it eats different kinds of food, it can be connected to several parts of the food web.

⚠️ What problems can it face?

Habitat disturbance, changes in food availability and conflict with people can create problems.

Food Omnivore
Habitat High mountain areas
Family Ursidae
Special feature Thick fur helps in cold conditions.

💡 Did you know?

Brown bears have a very strong sense of smell, which helps them find food.

THE PROBLEM

What is putting wildlife in danger?

Wildlife can face many different problems. Some are caused by direct human activities, while others are connected to larger environmental changes.

🌳

Habitat loss

When forests, wetlands or grasslands are destroyed or changed, animals can lose their food, shelter and places to reproduce.

🛣️

Habitat fragmentation

Roads and buildings can divide one large habitat into smaller pieces. This can make it harder for animals to move around.

🗑️

Pollution

Plastic, chemicals and polluted water can affect animals, plants and the places where they live.

🏹

Illegal hunting

Illegal hunting can reduce wildlife populations and remove important species from ecosystems.

🌡️

Climate change

Changes in temperature, rainfall and seasons can affect habitats and the availability of food and water.

👥

Human-wildlife conflict

When people and wild animals use the same areas, conflicts can sometimes happen.

SO WHAT CAN WE DO?

Wildlife conservation

Conservation means protecting wildlife and the places they need to survive. There is not just one solution.

🏞️

Protected areas

National parks and wildlife sanctuaries protect habitats and the species living there.

🌱

Restore habitats

Damaged forests, wetlands and other habitats can sometimes be restored.

🔬

Scientific research

Scientists study animals and ecosystems to understand what is happening and what can help.

📚

Education

When people understand wildlife better, they can make more informed choices.

🚫

Stop illegal hunting

Laws and proper enforcement can help reduce illegal hunting and wildlife trade.

🌳

Wildlife corridors

Connected habitats can give animals safer ways to move between areas.

💧

Protect water

Clean rivers, lakes, ponds and wetlands are important for many species.

♻️

Reduce waste

Using less plastic and creating less waste can reduce pressure on the environment.

THE BIG IDEA

Saving wildlife means saving the world around it too.

A tiger needs prey. Prey need plants. Plants need sunlight, water and soil. And all of them are part of one connected system. That is why wildlife conservation is not only about saving animals. It is about protecting the connections that keep ecosystems working.
