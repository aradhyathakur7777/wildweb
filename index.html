01 / The basics

First, what is an ecosystem?

An ecosystem includes living things and the non-living things around them, all interacting in one place. Plants, animals, sunlight, water, air and soil are all part of the picture.

What I find interesting is that changing one part of an ecosystem can affect other parts too. Nature is connected in ways we might not notice at first.

🌿

Living things

Plants, animals, fungi and tiny organisms are living parts of an ecosystem. They depend on their surroundings to survive.

☀️

Non-living things

Sunlight, water, air, rocks and soil are non-living parts. They still play a big role in supporting life.

🔗

Everything connects

Plants provide food and shelter, animals help spread seeds, and decomposers return nutrients to the soil.

Forest Ecosystem

Forests have trees, plants, animals and many other organisms. The trees provide shelter, while fallen leaves break down and add nutrients to the soil.

Something interesting: A fallen log can become a home for insects, fungi and other small organisms.
03 / Follow the energy

Who eats whom?

A food chain shows how energy passes from one organism to another. Most food chains begin with a producer, such as a green plant, which uses sunlight to make food.

🌱

1. Producer

Green plants make their own food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide through photosynthesis.

🐛

2. Consumer

A caterpillar may feed on a plant. A bird may then eat the caterpillar.

🍄

3. Decomposer

Fungi and bacteria break down dead matter and return nutrients to the environment.

Try following this chain: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

This is one example of a food chain. Real ecosystems usually have many connected food chains, forming a food web.

05 / What can go wrong?

Threats to wildlife

While learning about conservation, I found that wildlife faces several problems. Some are caused by human activities, and some become worse because of changes in the environment.

🪓

Habitat loss

Forests and other natural areas may be cleared for buildings, roads or farming. Animals can lose their food and shelter.

🗑️

Pollution

Plastic, chemicals and waste can pollute land and water. Animals may swallow plastic or become trapped in it.

🐾

Illegal hunting

Some animals are hunted or captured illegally. This can reduce their populations and disturb ecosystems.

🌡️

Climate change

Changes in temperature and rainfall can affect habitats, migration, food supplies and the survival of some species.

🔥

Forest fires

Uncontrolled fires can destroy habitats and harm wildlife. Some fires also release large amounts of carbon dioxide.

🌱

Invasive species

Species introduced to a new area can sometimes spread quickly and compete with native species for resources.

08 / About this project

Why I made WILDWEB

I made WILDWEB as part of my Biology project on the conservation of wildlife and ecosystems. I wanted to present the topic in a way that people could explore instead of only reading paragraphs on a page.

While preparing this project, I learned that every organism has a place in its ecosystem. Protecting wildlife also means looking after the habitats, food sources and natural resources that living things depend on.

I hope this project encourages people to notice the natural world around them and understand why conserving it matters.
